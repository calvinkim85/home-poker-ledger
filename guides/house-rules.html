House rules worth agreeing

Before the first hand
← Back to the ledger

Guides · Running the game

Most arguments at a home game are not about poker. They are about a rule nobody agreed to, discovered at the worst possible moment, with money already in the pot.

You do not need a rulebook. You need about ten decisions made out loud before the first hand, ideally in a message sent the day before so nobody is negotiating while holding cards.

The money rules

These cause the real disputes, because they are the ones with a number attached.

  • The buy-in. One fixed amount, agreed in advance. “Whatever you feel like” produces a table where nobody knows what anyone is risking.
  • Rebuys: allowed, and until when? “Unlimited until midnight” is a fine answer. So is “one each.” What causes trouble is not having answered. There is more on rebuy rules here.
  • Late arrivals. Can someone join at 10pm, and for how much?
  • Cashing out early. Can a player leave at midnight with their stack, or is there an agreed finish time?

That last one matters more than it looks. A big winner leaving at 11pm changes the night for everyone still playing, and a table that never discussed it will discuss it badly.

The table rules

Standard casino practice is a reasonable default, mostly because it is what any experienced player already expects.

  • Cards speak. The hand is what it is, regardless of what its owner announces it to be.
  • No string bets. State the amount, or move the chips in one motion. Announce first and you can never be accused of anything.
  • Acting out of turn is binding if it affects the action behind it. Most home games are forgiving here; agree which yours is.
  • A card off the table is dead, and a misdeal is called before significant action, not after someone sees their hand.
  • One player per hand. No coaching the person deciding.

The social rules nobody writes down

These are softer, and they are the ones that quietly decide whether people come back.

  • When does the game actually end? An agreed last hand beats a slow, resentful fade at 3am.
  • Phones at the table, or not.
  • Who is dealing — rotating button, or one person all night.
  • Food, drink, and who is paying for it. If the host covers everything, say so, so nobody is guessing.

Appoint someone to decide

The most useful rule is the one about rules: one person settles disputes on the night. Usually the host. Not because they are always right, but because a table of six people relitigating a ruling with a pot sitting there is how a good evening ends badly.

A decision that is slightly wrong and immediate beats a correct one arrived at twenty minutes later.

Write them once, reuse them

Put the list in the group chat and leave it there. New players read it before they arrive, which saves them asking, and it means changes are visible rather than discovered.

Keep it short. A list of six rules gets read. A page of twenty gets ignored, and an ignored rule is worse than no rule, because someone will cite it later.
